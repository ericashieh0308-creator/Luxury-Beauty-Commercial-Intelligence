# Executive brief | Premium beauty commercial intelligence

**Decision focus.** Separate catalog assortment and pricing signals from the financial decision to fund a promotion. This independent case uses licensed snapshots locally and a separately labeled, simulated retailer planning model.

| Evidence | Management use | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Observed catalog and review records | Assess assortment coverage, list-price architecture, source flags, and historical review topics | SKU and product counts measure catalog presence. They do not measure sales share or actual demand. |
| Candidate price pairs | Prioritize source verification for market and historical comparisons | Shared names or IDs alone cannot establish identical specifications. Leave quantitative differences unavailable until the evidence clears the match and price checks. |
| Simulated promotion economics | Set a minimum response hurdle before committing campaign spend | Inputs are illustrative. Actual demand response, returns, funding and landed costs require independent evidence. |

**Illustrative planning decision.** At the workbook defaults, an offer can add revenue and still reduce contribution after campaign cost. A 15% discount and 20% assumed volume uplift increase simulated net revenue by $6,110 and reduce simulated contribution by $455. Use this as a sensitivity demonstration. Before authorizing a real budget, obtain historical order and return data, landed costs, channel fees, funding terms, and a credible incrementality estimate. **Publication gate.** Run the current branch on the licensed local archives, review source quality and match evidence, and obtain permission before sharing any derived outputs. The public case can use the synthetic examples and simulated financial outputs.
